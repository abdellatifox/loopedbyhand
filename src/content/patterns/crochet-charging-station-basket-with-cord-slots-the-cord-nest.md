This crochet Charging Station Basket with Cord Slots, also known as The Cord Nest, is a stylish and practical way to organize charging cords, electronics, and small devices. It features unique cord slots for easy access, making it enjoyable to create something functional and neat. You'll be working mainly with single crochet stitches and some simple increases.

This pattern is suitable for anyone looking to enhance their crochet skills while making a useful item. Whether you’re new to crochet or have some experience, you'll be able to follow along and create a lovely storage solution for your cords.

## Why You'll Love This Charging Station Basket
- Stylish design – this basket is not only practical but also adds a touch of style to your space.
- Functional cord slots – easily access your charging cables without the mess.
- Quick to work up – perfect for those short on time who need a neat solution.
- Easy to personalize – play with colors or yarn types to make it truly yours.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! This pattern is straightforward and involves basic crochet techniques. You'll learn how to increase and create functional cord slots, making it a great project for expanding your skills.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- sc – single crochet
- sl st – slip stitch
- inc – increase (2 sc in same stitch)
- st(s) – stitch(es)

## Size and Gauge
- Finished dimensions: approximately 25 cm (10 in) in diameter and 18 cm (7 in) tall
- Gauge: 12 sc and 10 rows = 10 cm (4 in)

Make sure to check your gauge by working a small swatch of 20 sc in the round. Adjust your hook size if necessary to match the gauge.

## How to Crochet a Charging Station Basket (Step by Step)

### Step 1: Base
The base is worked in rounds, forming a flat circle to build upon. 
1. Ch 4, sl st in the first ch to form a ring.
2. Round 1: Ch 1, 8 sc in the ring. (8 sts)
3. Round 2: Ch 1, *inc* in each st around. (16 sts)
4. Round 3: Ch 1, *sc in next st, inc in next st* around. (24 sts)
5. Round 4: Ch 1, *sc in next 2 sts, inc in next st* around. (32 sts)
6. Round 5: Ch 1, *sc in next 3 sts, inc in next st* around. (40 sts)

### Step 2: Sides
The sides will rise from the base, giving the basket its shape. 
7. Round 6: Ch 1, sc in each st around. (40 sts)
8. Round 7: Ch 1, sc in each st around. (40 sts)
9. Round 8: Ch 1, sc in each st around. (40 sts)
10. Round 9: Ch 1, *sc in next 3 sts, ch 3 (to create a cord slot), skip next 3 sts, sc in each of the next 3 sts, ch 3* repeat around. (40 sts with cord slots) 
11. Round 10: Ch 1, sc in each st and ch around. (40 sts)

### Step 3: Finishing
Now we’ll narrow the top and finish off the basket. 
12. Round 11: Ch 1, sc in each st around. (40 sts)
13. Rounds 12–13: Repeat round 11 two more times. (40 sts)
14. Round 14: Ch 1, *sc in next 3 sts, sc2dec* around. (32 sts)
15. Round 15: Ch 1, *sc in next 2 sts, sc2dec* around. (24 sts)
16. Round 16: Ch 1, *sc in next st, sc2dec* around. (16 sts)
17. Round 17: Ch 1, sc2dec around. (8 sts)

Fasten off and leave a long tail for sewing.

## Assembly & Finishing
- Use the tapestry needle to weave in ends securely.
- Block the basket if needed to shape it properly.
- Make sure each cord slot is functional by leaving enough space for cords to be pulled through easily.

## Beginner Tips for a Great Charging Station Basket
- Use a stitch marker to keep track of rounds, especially in the sides where it can be easy to lose your place.
- Count your stitches after each round to ensure you haven’t missed any.
- Consider using a worsted yarn with a soft texture for added style and comfort.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- My stitches are too tight. – This can happen if you’re holding the yarn too tightly. Relax your grip and try again.
- The basket is uneven. – Make sure your stitch count is correct in each round; it helps keep everything balanced.
- The cord slots are too tight. – If the slots don’t allow cords to pass through easily, loosen your tension while making them or adjust the stitch count.

## How to Care for Your Charging Station Basket
Hand wash in cool water and lay flat to dry. Avoid bleach, as it can damage the fabric. 

## FAQ
### Can I use a different yarn?
Yes, you can use another yarn type, but be sure to check the gauge. Different yarn weights may alter the size and structure of your basket.

### How can I adjust the size of my basket?
To adjust the size, you can add more rounds in the base before transitioning to the sides. Just keep the stitch increases consistent.

### Will this basket hold larger cords and chargers?
Yes, as long as you design the cord slots to be wide enough, this basket can accommodate various cable sizes as you crochet.
