This crochet tote bag pattern makes a stylish and functional accessory with a unique hand warmer handle. The Warm Grip Tote is not only great for holding your daily essentials, but it also keeps your hands warm on chilly days while you’re out and about. You’ll be working with basic stitches like double crochet and single crochet, as well as some special techniques like back post and front post double crochet.

This project is perfect for crocheters looking to challenge themselves with intermediate skills. You’ll gain experience in working with different yarn colors and constructing a unique feature while creating a practical item you'll love using.

## Why You'll Love This Tote
- Stylish design – this tote adds a touch of flair to your everyday outings.
- Functional hand warmer – the unique handle keeps your hands cozy while carrying.
- Perfect for daily use – whether shopping or as a casual handbag, this tote has you covered.
- A great challenge – you'll improve your skills with double crochet and special stitches.

## Skill Level: Intermediate
To complete this pattern, you should have a grasp of basic stitches like single crochet and double crochet. You’ll also learn special stitches, such as back post and front post double crochet, which may take some practice but will enhance your crocheting abilities.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- st – stitch
- sl st – slip stitch
- dc – double crochet
- sc – single crochet
- bpdc – back post double crochet
- fpdc – front post double crochet

**Special Stitches:**
- **Back Post Double Crochet (bpdc):** Yarn over, insert hook from back to front around the post of the stitch below, yarn over again, pull through. Yarn over, pull through two loops, yarn over, pull through two loops again. 

## Size and Gauge
- **Finished Size:** Approximately 14 inches (35.5 cm) tall by 16 inches (40.6 cm) wide
- **Gauge:** 14 dc and 8 rows = 4 inches (10 cm) in double crochet (DC). To check gauge, crochet a square measuring 10 x 10 inches. Adjust by changing your hook size as necessary.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Part 1: The Tote Bag Body
The body is worked in rows to create a rectangular shape. 
1. Foundation Row: With MC, ch 60. (59 sts)
2. Row 1: DC in 4th ch from hook and in each ch across. Turn. (58 sts)
3. Row 2-20: Ch 3, DC in each st across. Turn. (58 sts)
4. Row 21: Ch 1, sc in each st across. Turn. (58 sts)
5. Row 22: Ch 3, DC in each st across. Turn. (58 sts)
6. Rows 23–25: Repeat Rows 21 & 22 once more. 

### Part 2: The Tote Bottom
Now, we’ll create the bottom of the tote for stability.
7. Row 26: Ch 1, sc in each st across. (58 sts)
8. Row 27: Turn work to begin on the bottom. Ch 1, sc in same st, sc in last st of the previous row. 
9. Row 28: Ch 1, sc in each st of the bottom (approx. 36 sts). 
10. Row 29: Turn and sc in the sides of the body to the top. 
11. Row 30: Finish with slip stitch to join. Weave in ends.

### Part 3: The Hand Warmer Handle
The handle is made separately and added for a cozy touch.
1. Switch to CC: Ch 8. 
2. Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook, then sc in each ch across. Turn. (7 sts)
3. Rows 2–10: Ch 1, sc in each st across. Turn. (7 sts)
4. Row 11: (First half) Ch 1, sc in the 1st two st, attach cotton batting, sc in remaining 5 st across.
5. Rows 12–15: Ch 1, sc across. Secure with a slip stitch.

### Assembly & Finishing
- **Joining Handle:** Attach the handle to one side of the tote using a whip stitch or slip stitch.
- **Edging:** Sc around the top of the tote with CC to tidy it up.
- **Weaving in Ends:** Weave in all loose ends with a tapestry needle.
- **Blocking:** If needed, block the tote to shape using blocking mats.

## Beginner Tips for a Great Tote
- **Check your gauge:** Ensuring the right gauge helps to achieve the desired size.
- **Count your stitches:** Keeping track prevents any unwanted surprises in your work.
- **Take breaks:** It’s okay to step away if you feel tired; crocheting is best done when you’re focused.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My stitches are too tight." – This might happen if you’re holding the yarn too tightly. Try relaxing your grip to make it easier.
- "The shape looks off." – If your rows aren’t matching up, you may have forgotten to turn your work. Always turn at the end of each row.
- "The handle feels too loose." – Ensure you attached the handle securely, and consider reinforcing it with extra stitches if necessary.

## How to Care for Your Tote
Hand wash your tote in cold water and lay it flat to dry. Avoid wringing it out, as this can distort its shape. 

## FAQ
### Can I use a different yarn?
Yes! Just make sure it’s a worsted weight yarn to maintain the correct size and structure.

### Can I adjust the size of the tote?
Absolutely! You can modify the foundation chain to create a larger or smaller tote, just remember to keep an even number for the stitches.

### How long will this take to make?
It should take approximately 3.5 hours total, depending on your speed and experience level.
